## 1. Vision

For Accucut Lasers' Admin, Leads and Engineers, who today prepare manufacturing cost estimates in one Excel worksheet (columns A to BO) that gives every holder of the file every rate and formula, the Accucut Quotation Portal is a role-based web application in which a Lead or Admin assigns an estimate, an Engineer enters part data through a guided form without ever seeing a cost, the portal calculates every cost column from master data exactly as the worksheet does, a Lead or Admin approves and locks a price, and the result exports to Excel in the worksheet's own layout. The Admin can also change fields and formulas in the portal with Excel-style re-lettering and an audit trail (built last in the initial release). It is valuable because it keeps pricing confidential, makes approval and late work visible, and removes dependence on a single spreadsheet and on a developer for each pricing change.

### 12.1 OQ-ARCH-10 / OQ-PM-07 as it goes to the delivery lead (DL-013; gate record condition 5)

For the HOLD-01 request, to be posted as a Jira open-question issue (playbook section 4.2) by the orchestrator, with the business-analyst's price questions. The product manager words the options; the business-analyst records the minimum for QTY per Assy in SPEC §7.1 once they answer, and the ux-expert then applies UX-36. The wording gives both options with their reasons, no recommendation and no reply text, and states the fallback. The example uses invented numbers (NFR-006).

> **A part with parts below it and a QTY per Assy of 0: what should the portal do?**
>
> **What happens today.** QTY per Assy is how many identical pieces of a part sit in one of its parent (your definition, AQP-13 comment 10487), and every level's quantity applies. So everything below a part is multiplied by that part's QTY per Assy. If an Engineer types 0 in QTY per Assy on a part that has parts below it, everything below it counts as 0.
>
> **An example with invented numbers.** An item with quantity 2 has a part with quantity 3, and below that part a sub-part with quantity 4 at a cost of 1 per piece. The total is 24. If the part's quantity is 0 instead of 3, the sub-part counts 0 times and the total is 0, with nothing on the Lead's screen to say why.
>
> **Why it needs a decision.** The written rules allow a 0 in any number field and say nothing more. Engineers have been told to enter 0 in a number that a part does not need (AQP-7), and a 0 is meant to change no calculation; for this field it does. An Engineer never sees a total, so the Engineer cannot see the effect either. We do not know of an assembly that really has 0 pieces, and we have not asked Accucut.
>
> **Block.** Calculate refuses the estimate and names the part: "QTY per Assy must be above 0 on a part that has parts under it". The Engineer fixes it and Calculate then goes through.
> - For: a 0 cannot silently remove the parts below it. A warning can be passed without being read.
> - Against: it stops an estimate where a 0 was meant, or where the count is not known yet, until a number is typed. A number typed only to get past the block (1, say) is a guess that nothing flags.
>
> **Warn.** Calculate shows a warning that names the part and says everything below it counts as 0, and the Engineer can go ahead.
> - For: nothing stops work where a 0 is meant or the count is not known yet. It matches how 0 is accepted in the other number fields.
> - Against: the warning can be passed without being read, and the Engineer cannot see what it does to the total, so a low price can reach the Lead. What the Lead would see is a detail we would settle with the screens.
>
> **Either way.** About 1 hour of work (the architect's estimate) and one message on one screen. It can be changed later. Within the team the architect favours Block and an earlier draft of the product requirements favoured Warn; the reasons above are theirs.
>
> **If we hear nothing.** We apply Block as a stop-gap by the delivery team, not as your decision, because a block cannot produce a wrong price and takes about 1 hour to undo, while a warning or no rule can leave a wrong price. We need this before the story for the total is ready to build (gate G4). You can change it at any time.
>
> **What we ask.** Which rule do you want, in your own words, and why in a sentence. Another rule is welcome too, for example a minimum other than 0.
